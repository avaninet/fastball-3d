Flight guide

Fastball 3D is a very generous and forgiving approximation of a space emergency scenario that has fortunately never occurred. Do not try this on orbit.

Your first pitch

The tool goes through the crosshair. You recoil the other way. On Tether separation, face away from the station before you throw. Look for the station in the aft camera. In the flight instruments, Current coast tells you whether you’ll reach the bars. Its directional hint tells you where to aim the next tool in the forward visor. Before your first single-use throw, check the recoil note beside Throw to see which way the pitch will push you relative to the station.

Choose a grab rule

In New flight, choose one of the six illustrated openings, then Straight-Line or Orbital. Any-speed is the default and accepts a grab at any speed. Try Firm (up to m/s) to practice braking, then Soft (up to m/s) for a gentler catch. For example, crossing the bars at 0.100 m/s earns a Firm grab. In Soft you’ll pass through unless you slow enough while still within reach. The suit uses the grab envelope for station contact and can coast through the station on an overspeed pass.

Tommi and the BOREX suit

Mission specialist Capt. Tommi Dylan (she/they) is widely rumored to have been the test pilot of the classified “unmanned” X-99 orbital spaceplane. “Let’s just say she’s set records she can never brag about.” — Commander Julian Bern

On Saturdays, the former high-school star pitcher still takes the mound with friends. Sometimes the next town comes over for a game.

Private space contractor BOREX designed its first spacesuit for servicing fleets of commercial datacenter satellites. The lightweight suit gives a technician more room to maneuver and is supposed to bring them home automatically. On its first outing, Tommi is out to cold-reboot an inference server when her tether parts and the thruster valves stick closed. Without either, a drifting astronaut is presumed irretrievable. Station commander Julian Bern still has her on radar.

Newton’s third law gives Tommi one last pitch. Fling a wrench out into space and the wrench pushes back on you. She grips the handle, loosens her fingers and grips again, just as she would a ball on Saturday. Ten minutes of reserve oxygen. It’s the night of the new moon. Earth hides sun and moon alike. A few stars hang above its dark curve as the station drifts farther away. Tommi looks down the throat of death. She’s going to pitch her way home. Bern would appreciate it if she could spare the drill. It’s practically her pet, and the crew only has the one.

Pitch from the forward visor

Begin flight starts the oxygen clock. Drag to turn or tap to center a bearing. Select a slot, adjust power and press Throw. Watch the tool leave just below the crosshair. A gentle toss stays in view longer. Turning alone leaves your velocity unchanged.

Preview a throw

Switch on Preview throw in the instrument panel to see where your suit would go if you released the selected tool now. The solid violet line and filled arrows update with your aim, tool and power. Farther arrows are smaller and dimmer. A violet cross marks the first contact or nearest pass. Looking straight down the path, you’ll see a diamond. The sidebar gives the projected grab, fast entry or closest clearance. Check aft when recoil takes you behind the crosshair. The dashed mint line shows your actual coast.

Pro tip: Work the problem — let’s not make things worse by guessing.

Manual (no guidance)

The BOREX suit has lost its relative navigation solution. The forward visor, aft camera, fixed crosshairs, oxygen display and equipment rack still work. Choose Manual in New flight or use the cockpit checkbox. Both Dodgeball scenarios support it. The clock holds your chosen speed through throws and close approaches. With an empty rack you coast until a grab, a debris strike or oxygen expiry.

Story still reloads tools and lets you aim while paused. First rescue and its lessons keep relative navigation and lesson coaching on. Start in Manual and keep it on for the entire attempt to earn Manual throughout on the flight card. Retry and challenge links restore the starting choice.

The night-side sky

Tommi is in Earth’s shadow. The Moon is new. On this pass, it lies below Tommi’s horizon along with the Sun. Look for the faint stars above Earth’s curved edge. They give you a reference as you turn the visor.

Read both cameras

STN points toward the station when it leaves the frame. The aft view looks straight behind you. A pair of amber bands marks one end of the station in both views. Mint arrows point along your committed coast. Nearby dashes are brighter and thicker. Farther arrows look smaller. Looking straight down a Straight-Line coast, you’ll see a ring and the distance from your suit to contact or to your position at oxygen expiry. If the path looks end-on, read the time, side and clearance under Current coast. A negative range rate means the gap to the bars is shrinking. Relative speed includes sideways motion. The gauge marks the chosen grab limit. Any-speed flights show the soft limit as a reference. Positive X points away from Earth, +Y along the orbit and +Z above its plane. The whole m outline (the thin side points along track in the standard orientation) is within reach, including the gaps between panels.

Choose Random station orientation in New flight to tilt the station for this opening. It holds that angle while you coast. Check the mint outline in both visors, especially when one view shows a thin edge. The miss distance and Preview throw use the tilted bars. X, Y and Z still follow the orbit.

Trim and brake

A shrinking range can end in a flyby. Current coast names the miss in station coordinates and tells you which way to aim in the forward visor. A pitch up and right nudges the suit down and left. If you reach the top or bottom of the visor, turn along the horizon toward the heading in the hint. On a future miss, match the estimated trim to the recoil beside Throw power. With single-use tools, the lightest remaining tool is selected after each release. Your power setting stays the same. Check both before you throw.

Fast entry in gives your projected speed at the bars. The gauge shows your speed now. Follow the mint DRIFT cue or press Face drift to aim along your motion. On a clear Firm or Soft approach, line up early and aim to brake in the final 30 seconds before entry. Fast-forward returns to 1× for that cue when you have a tool left. Press Throw to brake, then check Current coast again. If you enter fast, you can still brake inside the bars or circle back.

With relative navigation on, the flight ends when your last tool leaves the rack if the forecast shows a miss or an unavoidable fast entry. A later eligible grab keeps the clock running, including an Orbital slowdown inside the bars. A forecast debris collision plays out to impact. Restoring navigation with an empty rack checks the coast still ahead under the same rules.

Time and controls

Straight-Line holds velocity between releases. Orbital bends the coast in a rotating frame. Every flight starts with 10:00 of oxygen, including First rescue. Play speed stops at 1×, 10×, 20× and 30×. Throwing resets it to 1×. Pause to read instruments, then resume to aim. Arrow keys turn 2°, or 0.25° with Shift. The number row and numpad select tools 1–9. The two key positions to the right of P (US [ and ]) or numpad minus and plus move throw power one stop. Power starts at 95% and snaps to the nearest 5% stop from 10% to 100% as you drag. For a finer trim, choose a lighter tool. Enter throws from the visor, either slider or a cockpit checkbox. A focused slider keeps its arrow keys. Heading increases as you turn left. P controls the clock.

Compare rescues

Δv (delta-v) is how much more you could change your speed or direction with the tools aboard. Single-use flights start with 0.456 m/s available. A throw at any power spends that tool. You can throw again as soon as you select the next pitch. Your score is the budget still aboard at rescue. A lost suit brings home zero. In Story, compare throw count and grab speed. Grab speed includes sideways motion. A hard Any-speed catch can earn an extra line in the debrief.

Each opening has a radio call for each flight model. The braking lesson has its own call. Bring the original drill home to hear the Upmass Hero epilogue after the call. A Story reload gives you a replacement drill. Choosing Skip coaching makes the flight ineligible for the award.

Flying through debris

A platform mount gives way and takes Tommi’s tether anchor with it. The loose cover and bent frame drift independently. A hit to Tommi’s suit ends the flight. Both Dodgeball modes keep the same opening geometry in Story and single-use flight. Both flight models and all three grab rules work here.

With relative navigation on, follow the short trails to see where each piece is heading. An amber box surrounds the piece your current coast would hit. The box moves with it and extends 4 cm past each side. Steer your whole suit outside the box, including around the frame’s open middle. The blue × marks where your suit would first touch the box, with a countdown. Violet shows the predicted contact for a previewed throw. You can also slow down and let a piece cross ahead.

If the impact point leaves the forward view or sits behind a panel, the COVER HIT or FRAME HIT arrow points toward it. A collision warning takes priority over station braking. Fast-forward drops to 1× with up to 65 seconds to react. Dodgeball Coach can suggest a tool, power and bearing to clear both pieces. Use Preview throw to check where that dodge leaves you relative to the bars. When Current coast says a piece clears in a few seconds, hold the coast through the pass, then trim home.

Share an opening

Random Drift and Random Dodgeball each have 4,096 openings. Dodgeball draws from eight debris layouts. Choose Generate opening to try another. Paste a friend’s ten-character code or link in New flight to load the same opening, station angle, flight model, grab rule, inventory and starting aids. F1 codes start with relative navigation online. F2 codes start in Manual. Earlier seed formats belong to the builds that created them. The flight card prints the same code. Retry restores its starting settings.

Choose Flight card in the debrief to share your flown path. Both charts use the same along-track axis and scale. The upper chart shows radial motion and the lower chart shows cross-track motion. Match the numbered throws between them. A blue ring marks a pass across Y = 0 that appears inside the grab volume in the upper view but clears the bars in three dimensions. In Dodgeball, the card also gives the closest suit-to-debris gap and its time for each piece.

Story / guided rescue

First rescue starts a fresh Straight-Line lesson. Every Story throw reloads the selected tool. Suit mass stays constant, so repeating a pitch at the same power gives the same recoil. You can line up while paused and, with relative navigation on, snap to measured bearings. First rescue pauses for one halfway check. In later lessons, choose when to line up a correction. In these lessons, Throw starts or resumes the clock at 1×. Skip coaching gives you single-use tools for the rest of this assisted flight. After skipping, Retry starts a single-use opening eligible for a Δv score. Choose Story flight in setup to use these assists on another opening. Story starts closer to the bars in the four debris-free scenarios.

Upmass Hero

“Supply, cross that replacement drill off the manifest. Think you could send us some board games instead?”

Back on Earth, the drill gets a ticker-tape parade. Tommi has a game that Saturday.

“Captain Dylan, do you read? We have an advisory from BOREX-Houston. Message as follows: ‘Have you tried turning it off and on again?’”

Tommi replies in the calm that lies beyond calm: “Copy, Station. Cycling thrusters now.”

The valves un-stick. The main oxygen supply flows freely. It reminds her of her tortoiseshell cat Raspberry softly nudging her leg for attention. The commander calls out: “Captain Dylan? I don’t know what you did, but it worked. ”

Raspberry, I’m coming home.

A few moments after the heavy hit, Tommi wakes to Commander Bern’s voice in her headset.

“Tommi? Tommi, are you there? If you can hear me, that hit must have jiggled something loose.”

She hears the hiss first. The main oxygen supply is flowing freely again. A tap on the controls brings the stuck thrusters back to life. Tommi eases clear of the wreckage and steers back to the bars, where Bern reaches out to catch her.
