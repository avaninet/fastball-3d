Flight guide

Your first pitch

The tool goes through the crosshair. You recoil the other way. On Tether separation, face away from the station before you throw. Look aft to see where you’re going. In the flight instruments, Current coast tells you whether you’ll reach the bars. Its directional hint tells you where to aim the next tool in the forward visor.

Tommi and the BOREX suit

Mission specialist Capt. Tommi Dylan (she/they) broke Mach 10 in the classified SR-73. On Saturdays, the former high-school star pitcher still takes the mound with friends. Sometimes the next town comes over for a game.

BOREX designed its first spacesuit for servicing fleets of commercial datacenter satellites. It gives a technician more room to maneuver and is supposed to bring them home automatically. Tommi is out to cold-reboot an inference server when her tether parts and the thruster valves stick closed. Without either, a drifting astronaut is presumed lost. Station commander Julian Bern still has her on radar.

Newton’s third law gives Tommi one last pitch. Fling a wrench out into space and the wrench pushes back on you. She grips the handle, loosens her fingers and grips again, just as she would a ball on Saturday. Ten minutes of air. The station a little farther away with every second. Here in Earth’s shadow under a new moon, Tommi looks down the throat of death. She takes a breath. She’s going to pitch her way home. Bern would appreciate it if she could spare the drill. It’s practically her pet, and the crew only has the one.

Pitch from the forward visor

Begin flight starts the oxygen clock. Drag to turn or tap to center a bearing. Select a slot, adjust power and press Throw. Turning alone leaves your velocity unchanged.

The night-side sky

Tommi is in Earth’s shadow. The Moon is new. On this pass, it lies below Tommi’s horizon along with the Sun. Look for the faint stars above Earth’s curved edge. They give you a reference as you turn the visor.

Read both cameras

STN points toward the station when it leaves the frame. The aft view looks straight behind you. Mint arrows point along your committed coast. Nearby dashes are brighter and thicker. Farther arrows look smaller. Looking straight down a Straight-Line coast, you’ll see a ring and the distance from your suit to contact or to your position at oxygen expiry. If the path looks end-on, read the time, side and clearance under Current coast. A negative range rate means the gap to the bars is shrinking. Relative speed includes sideways motion. The gauge marks the 0.080 m/s soft limit, shown as a reference during any-speed flights. Positive X points away from Earth, +Y along the orbit and +Z above its plane. The whole 14 × 14 × 3.2 m outline is within reach, including the gaps between panels.

Trim and brake

A shrinking range can end in a flyby. Current coast names the miss in station coordinates and tells you which way to aim in the forward visor. If it says to aim up and right, a light pitch that way nudges the suit down and left. The hint follows you as you turn. Pitch along measured drift to brake. Soft capture takes effect anywhere inside the bars at 0.080 m/s or slower. After a fast entry, brake while still within reach or coast through and return. After the last throw, the flight ends if the coast misses the bars or every remaining chance to grab is too fast. If Orbital drift will slow you enough within reach, keep coasting.

Time and controls

Straight-Line holds velocity between releases. Orbital bends the coast in a rotating frame. Every flight starts with 10:00 of oxygen, including First rescue. Play speed stops at 1×, 10×, 20× and 30×. Throwing resets it to 1×. Pause to read instruments, then resume to aim. Arrow keys turn 2°, or 0.25° with Shift. Keys 1–9 select a tool, [ and ] change power, Enter throws from the visor, and P controls the clock.

Compare rescues

Δv (delta-v) is how much more you could change your speed or direction with the tools aboard. The debrief shows how much of the original budget you brought home. A loss brings home zero. Grab speed measures how gently you reached the bars, including sideways motion.

Share a random drift

In New flight, choose Random drift to generate a seed or load a friend’s code or replay link. Copy replay includes the flight model, capture rule and Story setting. In an offline copy, paste the shared replay into Load seed. The debrief also has a flight card you can save as an image.

Story / guided rescue

First rescue starts a fresh Straight-Line lesson. Story also lets you stage an opening, line up while paused and snap to measured bearings. Throw starts or resumes the clock at 1×. Choose Story flight in setup to use these assists on another opening.
