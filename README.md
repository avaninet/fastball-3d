## The service call

Private space contractor BOREX designed its first spacesuit for the people who would maintain fleets of commercial datacenter satellites. The lightweight suit gives a technician more freedom to maneuver, and its automatic return system is intended to bring them home if they come loose. On the first outing, **mission specialist Capt. Tommi Dylan (she/they)** - rumored test pilot of the classified "unmanned" X-99 orbital spaceplane, and former star pitcher of her high school baseball team - is making the orbital equivalent of a service call to cold-reboot an inference server. Yes, it takes all nine tools.

The tether parts. The thruster valves stick closed. Ten minutes reserve oxygen. An astronaut drifting away without a tether or working emergency thrusters is _presumed irretrievable_. Station commander Julian Bern can still see Tommi's radar track. Newton’s third law gives her one last option: Fling a wrench as hard as she can out into space and the wrench pushes her back.

It’s the night of the new moon. From here in Earth’s shadow, the planet hides sun and moon alike. A few stars hang above the dark curve of Earth, the station a little farther away with every second. Tommi looks down the throat of death and decides she’s going to pitch her way home.

## Fly your first rescue

1. Choose **First rescue** for a guided first pitch. To fly on your own, start with **Tether separation** and **Straight-Line**.
2. In a single-use flight choose **Begin flight** to aim and throw. Tap the aft camera or press **V** to turn around from the initial station view, then throw the spare battery at its starting power of 95%. The tool goes through the forward crosshair and your suit recoils the other way. Look for the station in the aft camera.
3. Check **Current coast**. **Contact in** means your present path will bring you to the bars. If it says **Miss by**, compare the recoil on the tool tiles as you change power, then use **Preview throw** to see where the correction would take you. Use **Play speed** to advance a settled coast. Open **Guide** in the cockpit for controls, braking and strategy.

## Choose a challenge

**New flight** has six openings. Try **Lateral drift** for a sideways approach, **Wide separation** for a longer return, or **Random Drift** for a step up. Its 4,096 curated separations can take several throws. In **Dodgeball Challenge**, a loose service cover and a bent frame cross your route home. **Random Dodgeball** offers eight layouts with 512 variations each.

Fly **Straight-Line** to coast in a straight line, or choose **Orbital** to see your path bend relative to the station. **Any-speed** lets you grab the bars at any speed. For a gentler arrival, **Firm** requires 0.120 m/s or slower and **Soft** requires 0.080 m/s or slower. **Story flight** gives you reloading tools and time to line up a pitch while paused.

Choose **Manual (no guidance)** to fly any opening by sight. The navigation display goes offline. Use the two cameras, oxygen clock and equipment rack. You control play speed, even during a close approach. Story still lets you line up while paused and reloads each tool. Start in Manual and keep it on, including while staged or paused, to earn **Manual throughout** on your flight card.

## Challenge a friend

Turn on **Random station orientation** in any of the six scenarios to try a different approach. The station holds its new angle for the whole flight. With navigation online, watch the mint outline in both visors to see how much room you have.

In **New flight**, use **Copy code** or **Copy link** for any of the six openings. For example, paste `F1B-5011G7` into **Challenge code or link** and choose **Load code** to fly a tilted, near-frame Random Dodgeball approach in Orbital with a Soft grab. The code restores Tommi, debris, station angle, flight model, capture rule, inventory and starting aids. F1 codes start with navigation online and F2 codes start in Manual. Older seed formats replay in the version that made them. Each player flies their own throws.

After the flight, choose **Flight card** in the debrief to save the image or copy the challenge. The upper chart shows radial motion and the lower chart shows cross-track motion. Match the numbered throws to follow the same pass in both views. Single-use wins lead with the percentage of Δv brought home. The flight log keeps the full route and precise times. Compare who made it home, who managed the gentlest grab, and who brought the drill back.

Fastball 3D is a very generous and forgiving approximation of a space emergency scenario that has fortunately never occurred. **Do not try this on orbit.**
